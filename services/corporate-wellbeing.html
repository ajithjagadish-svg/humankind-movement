Corporate and Community Wellbeing

Corporate and community wellbeing programmes give organisations customised, practical support that goes beyond generic wellness advice, helping teams reflect on stress, energy, movement, communication, and sustainable ways of working. The focus is practical, human, and grounded in self-understanding.

Programmes may focus on:

  • Movement and wellbeing.
  • Self-awareness and behaviour.
  • Neuro-inclusion.
  • Health as a foundation for participation.
  • Communication and trust.
  • Sustainable performance without burnout.

Built around your organisation

Humankind Movement designs customised wellness programmes for corporate teams, sports communities, residential communities, youth groups and educational spaces, and wellness communities, including postpartum recovery offered through HR wellness programmes and founder networks. As with individual coaching, the starting point is understanding the group and its context, not applying a generic wellness template.

The work is designed to help environments become more human.

Common questions

Can programmes be customised for our organisation?

Yes. Humankind Movement designs customised wellness programmes for corporate teams, sports communities, residential communities, youth groups and educational spaces, and wellness communities, including postpartum recovery offered through HR wellness programmes and founder networks.

Is postpartum recovery support available as part of a corporate wellness programme?

Yes, postpartum recovery is offered through HR wellness programmes and founder networks, alongside the other wellbeing focus areas.

What does a corporate wellbeing programme actually include — talks, coaching, or both?

It depends on what your organisation actually needs — programmes can include workshop-style group sessions, ongoing one-to-one or small-group coaching for individuals, or a mix of both. Rather than a fixed package of a talk plus a gym voucher, the format is built around your team's real context: what's affecting people day to day, and what kind of support would actually help, not a generic wellness perk.

How is this different from typical corporate wellness offerings, like yoga sessions, health talks, or gym memberships?

Most standard corporate wellness offerings — yoga classes, one-off health talks, gym memberships — are generic and optional add-ons layered on top of work as usual. This starts from a different place: understanding your team and its actual stressors, communication patterns, and health context before designing anything, so the programme addresses what's really affecting people rather than checking a wellness-perk box. It's built to change how a team functions day to day, not just offer an activity.

Can this address burnout and sustainable performance, not just general fitness?

Yes, that's one of the core focus areas: sustainable performance without burnout, alongside movement, self-awareness, communication, and health as a foundation for participation. The approach treats energy, stress, and movement as connected rather than addressing fitness in isolation from how a team is actually working, so a programme can center on burnout and sustainable pace specifically if that's what your organisation needs most right now.

Does this work for remote or hybrid teams, or only in-person?

Yes. Most coaching and programme delivery happens online and works well for distributed, remote, or hybrid teams, alongside in-person sessions where that fits better for a specific team or location. The right mix of online and in-person gets shaped around your organisation's actual setup during the initial conversation, rather than assuming everyone is in one office.

How is a corporate programme priced, and what determines the cost?

There's no fixed package price — cost depends on factors like group size, programme scope (a single workshop versus ongoing coaching), duration, and format (in person or online). Rather than quoting a generic rate card, pricing is worked out based on your organisation's actual context and needs, which is why the first step is a conversation about what you're looking for, not a pricing page.

What outcomes can we expect to measure or report on to leadership?

Outcomes are tied to the areas a programme actually focuses on — things like communication and trust, self-awareness and behaviour, sustainable performance without burnout, and participation — rather than a generic fitness metric. What's specifically tracked and how it's reported depends on your organisation's goals and what leadership actually wants visibility into, and that gets discussed and set up as part of designing the programme, not applied as a fixed template.
