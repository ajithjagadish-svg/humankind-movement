Common questions

Is this guide safe to follow without seeing a doctor first?

This guide is educational content built from published research, not a substitute for medical or physiotherapy care, and it says so directly inside. If anything about your recovery feels off — pain, unusual bleeding, or something that doesn't match what's described — that always deserves a real clinical assessment first, not a guide. The guide is designed to work alongside your medical clearance and care team, filling the gap between being cleared as safe and actually knowing how to move again, not replacing that clearance.

Is the guide medically reviewed, or just general fitness advice?

It's reviewed, not just general advice. The guide has been reviewed by Dr. Teji Dawane, MBBS, MS (OBG), Head of Department and Lead Consultant, Obstetrics and Maternal Medicine at Medicover Women and Children Hospital, Bengaluru, who specifically notes it fills the gap between medical clearance and actually knowing how to move again, alongside a mother's care, not instead of it. It's built from published research, not opinion or trend-based fitness content.

How long is the guide, and what exactly does it cover?

It's a focused, practical read, not a long manual; it covers why "bouncing back" is the wrong goal, what a diastasis recti gap actually does and doesn't tell you, the pelvic floor beyond Kegels, a separate recovery timeline for C-section, a realistic evidence-based return-to-exercise timeline, why sleep and stress are part of physical recovery, a simple self-check before you start moving again, four breathing exercises you can try right away, and the bounce-back myths worth letting go of.

Will signing up enrol me in a coaching programme, or send me a lot of emails?

No. Signing up only gets you the guide by email immediately, plus occasional posts like it, not a coaching enrolment or a heavy email sequence. Your details aren't shared with anyone else. If you're interested in coaching after reading the guide, that's a separate step you'd take yourself, not something signing up here commits you to.

Is this guide relevant if I had a C-section, or is it only for vaginal birth?

It covers both. The guide includes a separate recovery timeline specifically for C-section, since healing from abdominal surgery follows a different path than vaginal birth recovery, rather than treating postpartum recovery as one identical timeline regardless of birth type.

Do I need to already have pain or diastasis to benefit from this guide, or is it useful for anyone postpartum?

It's useful either way. If you're dealing with diastasis, pain, or uncertainty about what's normal, the guide addresses that directly. If you haven't noticed specific issues yet, it's still relevant because it covers what clearance actually means versus readiness, a self-check before you start moving again, and habits like sleep and stress that affect recovery whether or not you have symptoms — most of what's inside is about understanding your own recovery, not treating a specific problem.
